Design (what gets printed)

MaterialPLA or PETG, single material, one print
Coupon120 × 120 mm, 53.6 mm deep
Front leaf0.8 mm thick, 0.5 mm holes on a 3 mm grid (1600 holes, σ ≈ 2.2%; 231 of them sit on a rib and are blocked)
Air gap 125 mm, divided by 1.2 mm ribs on a 20 mm grid
Rear leaf0.8 mm thick, 0.5 mm holes on a 6 mm grid (400 holes, σ ≈ 0.55%; 76 of them sit on a rib and are blocked)
Air gap 225 mm, same ribbed grid
Back wall2 mm solid
Print0.4 mm nozzle, 0.1 mm layers, front leaf on the bed, 3 perimeters, no top/bottom gaps in the leaves. Enable hole compensation or drill-check the 0.5 mm holes.

Why two leaves: one perforated plate gives a narrow peak. Two leaves with different hole spacing stagger their resonances, which spreads the absorption across the band. In the model the rear leaf takes about 36% of the incident energy across the band and the front leaf about 29%.

Limits of this proof of concept

  • The curves use the exact viscous tube (Bessel) impedance of each hole at normal incidence, with an end correction of 0.85 d. The older Maa (1998) fit gave 0.56 and is kept for comparison. The 3D view shows the 1-D field, not a full 3-D simulation of the hole mouths.
  • The band average is about 0.65, with dips near 300 Hz and above 2.5 kHz. A realistic target is a broad mid-band absorber, not a flat 1.0 across 300–3000 Hz. Filling the edges needs a deeper cavity or a third leaf.
  • 0.5 mm holes are at the limit of a 0.4 mm nozzle. Print a test strip first and measure the real hole diameter.
  • About 14% of the front-leaf holes and 19% of the rear-leaf holes sit on a rib and are blocked. The model ignores this; with them removed the band average drops only to about 0.64, but that is wasted area.
  • The end correction (0.85 d) is a standard value, not measured here. Changing it by ±0.4 d moves the band average by a few hundredths.
  • Validate before trusting the numbers: test a printed coupon in an impedance tube (ASTM E1050 or ISO 10534-2).
