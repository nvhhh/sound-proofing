Design (what gets printed)

MaterialPLA or PETG, single material, one print
Coupon120 × 120 mm, 53.6 mm deep
Front leaf0.8 mm thick, 0.5 mm holes on a 3 mm grid (1600 holes, σ ≈ 2.2%; 231 of them sit on a rib and are blocked)
Air gap 125 mm, divided by 1.2 mm ribs on a 20 mm grid
Rear leaf0.8 mm thick, 0.5 mm holes on a 6 mm grid (400 holes, σ ≈ 0.55%; 76 of them sit on a rib and are blocked)
Air gap 225 mm, same ribbed grid
Back wall2 mm solid
Print0.4 mm nozzle, 0.1 mm layers, front leaf on the bed, 3 perimeters, no top/bottom gaps in the leaves. Enable hole compensation or drill-check the 0.5 mm holes.

Why two leaves: one perforated plate gives a narrow peak. Two leaves with different hole spacing stagger their resonances, which spreads the absorption across the band. In the model the rear leaf takes about 33% of the incident energy across the band and the front leaf about 23%.

Limits of this proof of concept

  • The curves come from the Maa (1998) micro-perforated-panel model at normal incidence. The 3D view shows that 1-D field, not a full 3-D simulation of the holes.
  • The band average is about 0.56, with dips near 300 Hz and above 2.5 kHz. A realistic target is a broad mid-band absorber, not a flat 1.0 across 300–3000 Hz. Filling the edges needs a deeper cavity or a third leaf.
  • 0.5 mm holes are at the limit of a 0.4 mm nozzle. Print a test strip first and measure the real hole diameter.
  • About 14% of the front-leaf holes and 19% of the rear-leaf holes sit on a rib and are blocked. The model ignores this; with them removed the band average stays at about 0.56, but that is wasted area.
  • The model has no end correction. Adding about 0.85 d (0.4 mm) to the plate thickness raises the band average to about 0.58, so the true value is uncertain by a few hundredths.
  • Validate before trusting the numbers: test a printed coupon in an impedance tube (ASTM E1050 or ISO 10534-2).
