Design (what gets printed)

MaterialPLA, PETG or ABS (nylon's leaf mode falls into the band; see README)
Coupon120 × 120 mm, 92 mm deep (incl. 5 mm back)
Front leaf1.0 mm thick, 0.5 mm holes on a 2.5 mm pitch (2209 holes, σ ≈ 3.1%)
Air gap 125 mm, divided by 1.2 mm ribs on a 20 mm grid
Rear leaf1.0 mm thick, 0.5 mm holes on a 5 mm pitch (576 holes, σ ≈ 0.8%)
Air gap 260 mm, same ribbed grid. Each 20 mm cell ends in a 4 mm bore, 5 mm long neck through the back wall (Helmholtz, tuned to about 120–250 Hz)
Back wall5 mm solid (dowel sockets and screw bosses)
Print0.4 mm nozzle, 0.1 mm layers, front leaf on the bed, 3 perimeters, no top/bottom gaps in the leaves. Drill-check the 0.5 mm holes; a 0.4 mm wall separates every hole from a rib.

Why two leaves: one perforated plate gives a narrow peak. Two leaves with different hole spacing stagger their resonances, which spreads the absorption across the band. In the model the rear leaf takes about 39% of the incident energy across the band and the front leaf about 27%.

Product line: three tiles and a 240 mm bed mix

Each product is a 120 mm tile. The bed mix is four tiles on a 240 mm print bed, chosen to keep the worst band as high as possible from 125 Hz to 4 kHz.

Limits of this proof of concept

  • The curves use the exact viscous tube (Bessel) impedance of each hole at normal incidence, with an end correction of 0.68 d, calibrated to a resolved 2-D simulation of one hole. The older Maa (1998) fit gave 0.56 and is kept for comparison. The 3D view shows the 1-D field, not a full 3-D simulation of the hole mouths.
  • The band average is about 0.66, with dips near 300 Hz and above 2.5 kHz. A realistic target is a broad mid-band absorber, not a flat 1.0 across 300–3000 Hz. Filling the edges needs a deeper cavity or a third leaf.
  • 0.5 mm holes are at the limit of a 0.4 mm nozzle. Print a test strip first and measure the real hole diameter.
  • No hole touches a rib: every hole keeps at least 0.4 mm of wall to a rib, so the printed holes match the model.
  • The end correction (0.68 d) comes from a 2-D resolved simulation, not measured here. Changing it by ±0.4 d moves the band average by a few hundredths.
  • Validate before trusting the numbers: test a printed coupon in an impedance tube (ASTM E1050 or ISO 10534-2).
